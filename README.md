## Experiment design

| Element | Choice |
|---|---|
| Intervention | Plain-language statement + QR mobile payment + 3-click payment plan |
| Unit of randomization | Patient (26,650 patients, 50,000 statements); no patient appears in both arms |
| Primary metric | Days from statement to settlement (paid statements) |
| Secondary | Paid within 30 days; unpaid at 120 days; QR/mobile share of paid statements |
| Guardrail | Dispute rate must not increase |
| Power | ~2,300 statements/arm detects a 1.2-day difference (SD ~14.5, alpha 0.05, power 0.80) |

## Results (simulated)

| Metric | Control | Treatment | Difference | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sample ratio (patients) | 13,227 | 13,423 | 50/50 check | 0.230 (pass) |
| Pre-treatment balance | $216.87 | $217.54 | n.s. | 0.692 |
| **Days to pay (primary)** | 49.49 | 42.36 | **-7.13 d** (95% CI 6.87 to 7.39) | < 0.001 |
| Days to pay, patient-level | | | -7.05 d | < 0.001 |
| Paid within 30 days | 8.65% | 17.83% | +9.18 pp | < 0.001 |
| Unpaid at 120 days | 11.61% | 8.09% | -3.52 pp | < 0.001 |
| Dispute rate (guardrail) | 3.30% | 2.76% | -0.54 pp | < 0.001 (not worse) |
| QR/mobile share (paid) | 22.1% | 52.6% | | |

Tests: Welch's t-test (primary), Welch on patient-level means (clustering robustness), Mann-Whitney U
(skew robustness), two-proportion z-tests (rates), chi-square (sample ratio, age balance).

## Illustrative business case

Scenario assumptions (editable in `run_analysis.py`): 120,000 statements/year,
8% cost of capital. These are **not additive**.

| Component | Type | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Cash collected earlier | One-time | $509,183 |
| Carrying-cost saving on that cash | Annual | $40,735 |
| Balances no longer unpaid at 120 days (gross, before collection costs) | Annual | $917,775 |

## Limitations

- Effects are designed into the simulation; real results would be smaller and noisier.
- The treatment bundles three changes; a factorial follow-up would isolate each.
- Dropping unpaid statements from the days-to-pay mean ignores censoring; a survival model is the production-grade approach.


Every number in the dashboard, infographic, notebook, and README is read from `results.json`; none is typed by hand.
